# Research Evidence Synthesis

## Role Family

`researcher`

## When to Use

- Use when multiple findings need to become one coherent evidence summary.
- Use when decision-makers need confidence and gaps called out clearly.
- Use when research should stay grounded in cited evidence.

## When Not to Use

- Do not use when the task is pure implementation.
- Do not use when there is only one trivial source and no synthesis is needed.
- Do not use to overstate weak or conflicting evidence.

## Inputs / Context Needed

- Research question
- Source findings or notes
- Any required date, scope, or citation constraints

## Core Rules / Steps

1. State the main answer in one or two sentences.
2. Group evidence by claim, not by reading order.
3. Separate confirmed evidence from inference.
4. Call out conflicts, missing data, and freshness limits.
5. End with the practical takeaway for the decision at hand.

## Constraints / Guardrails

- Do not blur evidence and opinion.
- Do not hide uncertainty behind polished prose.
- Prefer fewer high-signal claims over exhaustive dumping.

## Validation Source

- Seeded from Paperclip research and evidence-formatting needs.

## Retire / Supersede When

- Retire when a domain-specific research synthesis skill replaces this general format.
